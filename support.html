Yo Noah helps a child with their daily routines – getting dressed, brushing teeth, packing a school bag – with gentle reminders, simple steps, photo checks, rewards and encouragement from the grown-ups in their family. I built it for my son Noah. This page explains how to set it up and get the best from it.

Watch the 3-minute introduction: open Yo Noah and tap Watch how it works on the first screen, or go to Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Help → Getting Started video.

1. Before you start

  • You need at least two iPhones: one for your child and one for you. Up to four grown-ups (called Reviewers) can each join your Yo Noah Family on their own iPhone.
  • Each iPhone needs iOS 18 or later, and Yo Noah installed.
  • Each iPhone must be signed in to iCloud (Settings → your name). Yo Noah keeps its photos and information in the iCloud account of the parent who sets up the Family (see Photos & data) – there are no Yo Noah accounts, passwords or servers. Apple Family Sharing is not needed.
  • Allow notifications when Yo Noah asks, on every iPhone. Alerts and reviews depend on them.

2. Try the Demo

Want to look around first? On the first screen tap Try the Demo. It opens a sample family – a child called Sam and a parent called Mum – on just this iPhone. Use the Switch to Child and Switch to Parent buttons in the banner at the top of the screen to try both roles. Nothing in the Demo is shared or saved to iCloud. When you're ready, tap Leave Demo and set up your real Family.

3. Set up your Family

  1. On your iPhone (a grown-up's), open Yo Noah and tap Set Up a New Family.
  2. Enter your child's first name – it's used throughout the app, for example “Yo Noah!” and “Noah's Day”. No other details about your child are needed.
  3. Enter your own name (and, if you like, your email address). You become the first Reviewer.

The iPhone you set up the Family on becomes the Family's owner: everything is stored in its iCloud account (see Photos & data), so it can't be removed from the Family. If you replace it, sign in to your new iPhone with the same Apple ID and reinstall Yo Noah – your Family comes back.

4. Add the child's and other grown-ups' iPhones

  1. On your iPhone go to Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Family & iPhones → Add an iPhone.
  2. Choose the role for the new iPhone: the Child's iPhone or a Reviewer's iPhone. A QR code appears.
  3. On the new iPhone, open Yo Noah, tap Join a Family and scan the code.
  • Each QR code works once and expires after 10 minutes. If it expires, just make a new one.
  • To add another grown-up – a second parent or a grandparent, for example – first go to Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Reviewers and add them as a new Reviewer (name and phone number). Then make a Reviewer QR code for their iPhone.
  • An iPhone's role can't be changed after it joins. To change it, remove the iPhone in Family & iPhones and add it again.

5. Create Alerts

An Alert is what tells your child a Task is starting. Set up and change Alerts in Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Alerts.

  • Picture: choose a photo from your library – on the child's iPhone it appears on a calm card that slides up with a gentle glow. Nothing flashes.
  • Voice recording: you might want an Alert to include a short message in your own voice to prompt your child, for example “Time to get dressed!”
  • Notification sound: if Yo Noah isn't open or the iPhone is locked, the Alert arrives as a notification with a sound. Choose Rising Bells (the default), one of five other sounds, or your own voice recording if it's 30 seconds or shorter. Tap a sound to hear it.
  • Include Countdown: shows a ring with the minutes left. It's on by default – switch it off if a countdown makes your child anxious.

An Alert has no time of its own – it plays when a Task using it starts. One Alert can be shared by several Tasks.

6. Create Rewards

To set up Rewards for your child, go to Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Rewards. A Reward can be anything – a cinema trip, choosing dessert, a small treat – or YouTube Time, which plays only inside Yo Noah.

  • Approved Tasks needed (1 to 5): how many approved Tasks earn the Reward. Your child sees yellow lightning bolts fill up as they get closer.
  • Late tasks are still rewarded: on by default. Switch it off if a Task must be done on time to count.
  • YouTube Time: set the number of minutes. Earned minutes go into your child's YouTube Time balance and don't expire.
  • When any other Reward is earned, it appears in the Needs You section of every Reviewer's Home Screen until one of you taps Given to show it has been handed over.

7. Create Tasks

Go to Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Tasks and add a Task:

  • Name – for example “Get Dressed for School”.
  • Steps – one to five short steps, for example “Lay out your clothes”, “Put your school clothes on”, “Put your pyjamas on your bed”.
  • Schedule – when the Task starts: one-off, or repeating on chosen days, dates of the month, or patterns like “first Monday of the month”.
  • Time Allowed – 15 minutes unless you change it. After that the Task shows as Late, with a calm message – your child can still finish it.
  • Alert – which Alert plays when the Task starts.
  • Reviewers (optional) – who is told about this Task and can approve it as done.
  • Reward (optional) – which Reward this Task counts towards.
  • Photo (optional) – your child takes a photo of the finished Task, and you check it. Add a short instruction saying what the photo should show, such as “Your pyjamas folded on your bed”. Photos stay inside Yo Noah (not in the photo library), are stored in the iCloud of the parent who set up the Family, and are deleted after a month.

Changes appear on the other iPhones within seconds. To pause a Task for a holiday without deleting it, make it Inactive: open the Task and pause it indefinitely or until a date, or swipe right on it in the Tasks list and tap Pause. To delete a Task, swipe left on it in the same list.

8. What your child sees

  • A simple Home Screen with today's Tasks, their Rewards progress and a YouTube Time button. There are no settings on the child's iPhone, so nothing can be changed by accident.
  • When a Task starts, the Alert plays and the Task is highlighted.
  • Opening a Task shows its steps to tick off one by one, the time remaining, and then Task Complete. If the Task needs a photo, a final step Take a Photo of the finished task! is added.
  • Leave for Today lets your child put a Task aside without fuss – you'll be told, and you can still reward the effort.
  • Streaks and “Your Best Ever” celebrate Tasks done day after day.

9. Reviewing and approving

Your Home Screen shows Needs You (anything waiting for you), your child's Day (every Task and its status) and Rewards (progress). You'll get a notification when a photo is ready, a Task is Late, a Task is left for today, or a Reward is earned.

When a photo arrives, choose:

  • Approve – the Task counts towards its Reward.
  • Ask for Another Photo – the Task is done but the photo didn't show what you needed. Pick or type a short, kind message.
  • Encourage Another Attempt – the Task isn't finished yet; your child is asked to have another go.

If a Task has more than one Reviewer, the first to act decides, and everyone sees who did. Tap any Task in your child's Day to see its photos, history and messages. Pull down on the Home Screen to check for changes straight away.

10. Sending encouragement

Tap Encourage beside your child's Day. Choose a message such as “You've got this!”, “Great job!”, “Nearly there!” or “I'm proud of you”, or write your own (up to 80 characters, emoji welcome). Send it about one Task or as a general message. It appears on your child's iPhone as a calm card with your name, and you'll see “✓ seen” once they've read it.

11. YouTube Time

Every family has its own approach to YouTube and social media. In mine, the children don't have YouTube or TikTok on their phones just yet – but they love it, and some time to watch YouTube and have a non-stop giggle over silly videos is a powerful incentive. So I built YouTube Time into Yo Noah as a very personal reward for Noah. It may or may not suit your family, but I hope this explains why it works the way it does. Whether you use it as a Reward is entirely up to you.

  • Your child can use their YouTube Time balance from the button on their Home Screen. The countdown only runs while a video is playing, with a gentle warning two minutes before the end.
  • Videos play inside Yo Noah using YouTube's official player. Searches use strict SafeSearch, age-restricted videos are hidden and videos made for kids are listed first. Change these in Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → YouTube filters.
  • Set No YouTube Time periods in Manage – for example weekdays 7:00 to 8:45 before school – each with its own short message. Any unused minutes stay in the balance.
  • Searches and videos go to YouTube (Google) – see the Privacy Policy.

Making sure YouTube can only be watched through Yo Noah

Yo Noah works alongside Apple's Screen Time. You can block YouTube everywhere else on your child's iPhone and YouTube Time still works inside Yo Noah. On your child's iPhone (or on yours, under Settings → Screen Time → your child's name, if you manage their Screen Time through Apple Family Sharing):

  1. Delete the YouTube app from your child's iPhone, if it's installed.
  2. Go to Settings → Screen Time, turn Screen Time on and set a Screen Time passcode that your child doesn't know.
  3. Tap Content & Privacy Restrictions and turn it on.
  4. To stop the YouTube app being reinstalled, tap iTunes & App Store Purchases → Installing Apps → Don't Allow. (This stops all new apps being installed until you change it back.)
  5. To block the YouTube website, tap App Store, Media, Web & Games (called Content Restrictions on some versions of iOS) → Web Content → Limit Adult Websites. Under Never Allow, tap Add Website and add youtube.com, www.youtube.com and m.youtube.com.

Don't block youtube-nocookie.com – that's the address Yo Noah's video player uses. Menu names can differ slightly between versions of iOS.

12. Tips for getting started

  • Start small. Begin with one or two Tasks your child already knows, but needs some support to do. Add more once a routine – and a positive feeling about Yo Noah and its Rewards – is established.
  • Keep Yo Noah running in the background on every iPhone – don't swipe it away – so Alerts, photos and messages arrive when they're sent.
  • Try your own voice for Alerts and see what works for your child. A familiar voice can be calmer than a beep, or a more effective prompt to start the Task.
  • Keep steps short – a few words each, one action per step.
  • Approve promptly at first, so the lightning bolts fill up while the Task is still fresh.

13. Troubleshooting

If Alerts or notifications don't arrive

  • Check Yo Noah wasn't swiped away in the app switcher – open it again and leave it running.
  • Settings → Notifications → Yo Noah: Allow Notifications on; on the child's iPhone also turn on Time Sensitive Notifications so Alerts show during Do Not Disturb and other Focus modes.
  • Sounds play at the ringer volume. On silent the iPhone vibrates instead – set Settings → Sounds & Haptics to vibrate in silent mode.

If changes don't appear on another iPhone

  • Check both iPhones are online and signed in to iCloud.
  • Open Yo Noah on both. On a Reviewer's Home Screen, pull down to check straight away.
  • The child's iPhone keeps working without internet and catches up when it reconnects.

If the QR code won't scan or says it has expired

  • Codes work once and last 10 minutes – make a new one in Family & iPhones.
  • Allow camera access for Yo Noah (Settings → Yo Noah → Camera).
  • Only one child's iPhone can be in a Family. To move to a new one, use Replace Child's iPhone.

If YouTube shows “Website Not Allowed”

That's Screen Time stopping your child leaving the video player for the YouTube website (for example by tapping the YouTube logo). The video itself keeps working inside Yo Noah.

If a photo or recording can't be added

Yo Noah asks for camera, microphone and photo library access only when needed. If you said no, turn it on in Settings → Yo Noah.

14. Changing or leaving your Yo Noah Family

  • Change your child's name: Manage (the ⚙︎ icon) → Family & iPhones.
  • New phone for your child: Replace Child's iPhone – all history, photos and Reward progress move across.
  • Lost or replaced Reviewer iPhone: remove it in Family & iPhones, then add the new one with a new QR code.
  • Remove Child from Family permanently deletes your child's history, photos and Reward progress from every iPhone (you're asked twice). Tasks, Alerts, Rewards and Reviewers are kept.

15. Photos & data – where they're kept

All the photos and information created in Yo Noah are stored in the iCloud account of the parent whose iPhone set up the Family (the Family's owner). They are then shared, through Apple's iCloud sharing, with every iPhone that has joined the Family – your child's and any other Reviewer's. No data is sent to any other server, except YouTube, which receives only your child's video searches and the videos they choose to watch (see YouTube Time).

How it works

  • When you tap Set Up a New Family, Yo Noah creates the Family in a private area of that iPhone's iCloud account.
  • Every other iPhone that joins with a QR code is given access to that same area. It does not get its own copy in its own iCloud account.
  • When your child takes a photo of a finished task, it goes straight into the owner's iCloud – not into your child's iCloud, and not into another Reviewer's iCloud, even if they are the one who reviews it.
  • Each iPhone keeps a local copy of what it shows (on the iPhone itself, not in its iCloud) so Yo Noah keeps working without internet. That copy is removed when the photo is deleted after a month, or when the iPhone leaves the Family.

What this means for you

  • Storage: photos count against the owner's iCloud storage. They're small and deleted after a month, so the effect is very small.
  • Privacy: your child's Apple ID never holds anyone else's information. If your child's iPhone is removed from the Family, its access stops and nothing is left in its iCloud account.
  • Losing the owner's iPhone: nothing is lost – it's all in the owner's iCloud. Signing in to the new iPhone with the same Apple ID and reinstalling Yo Noah brings it back.
